STANDARD ICC ARBITRATION CLAUSE
(English Version)
Source: https://iccwbo.org/news-publications/arbitration-adr-rules-and-tools/standard-icc-arbitration-clauses-english-version/

================================================================================

STANDARD CLAUSE

"All disputes arising out of or in connection with the present contract shall be
finally settled under the Rules of Arbitration of the International Chamber of
Commerce by one or more arbitrators appointed in accordance with the said Rules."

================================================================================

GUIDANCE ON OPTIONAL ADDITIONS

Parties may wish to stipulate in the arbitration clause itself:

(a) the number of arbitrators
    Note: The ICC Rules provide that, absent an agreement of the parties on the
    number of arbitrators, the ICC Court shall appoint a sole arbitrator, save
    where it appears that the dispute is such as to warrant the appointment of
    three arbitrators.

(b) the seat (or legal place) of the arbitration

(c) the language of the arbitration

(d) the law governing the substance of the dispute (i.e. the governing law of
    the contract)

================================================================================

EXAMPLE OF A MORE COMPLETE CLAUSE

"All disputes arising out of or in connection with the present contract shall be
finally settled under the Rules of Arbitration of the International Chamber of
Commerce by [one arbitrator / three arbitrators] appointed in accordance with
the said Rules.

The seat of the arbitration shall be [city, country].

The language of the arbitration shall be [language].

The contract shall be governed by the substantive law of [jurisdiction]."

================================================================================

IMPORTANT NOTE

Parties should take care not to modify the standard clause in a way that could
create ambiguity, cause delay, or compromise the dispute resolution process.
Parties should also ensure that the clause meets enforceability requirements
under applicable law at both the seat of arbitration and the anticipated place(s)
of enforcement.
